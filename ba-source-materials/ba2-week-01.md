  Univariate Analysis

●  A technique used to analyze a single INDEPENDENT* variable or a single DEPENDENT
  variable
●  This is the simplest way of analyzing data
●  “Uni” = one
●  Aim to generate a summary that describes patterns associated with the data.
●  Not examining relationships or causes.
●  Patterns and relationships are not the same thing!
*also known as the predictor variable

---

  Descriptive
  Statistics
•  Help describe the basic features of the
  sample data
•  Provide simple summaries of the sample
•  Broken down into measures of tendency
  (mean, median, mode) and measures of
  spread (Standard/Deviation, ranges,
  quartiles, absolute deviation, Variance)

---

  For example: Clothing Store
  Imagine you own a clothing store, and you are keeping track of monthly sales and
  inventory
  What things would you want to know?
•  Important statistics would be:
•  The average (mean) sales for that month
•  The top item sold that month (mode)

Very useful for big data sets, where it would be hard to visualize data trends without descriptive
  statistics!

  However, with descriptive statistics alone, you cannot make conclusions beyond the data
  analyzed – they are just used to describe the data

  Quartiles
  Quartiles separate the data set in to quarters, or quartiles

•  First quartile, or lower quartile (Q1): below this point, is one quarter of the data.
•  Second quartile, or median (Q2): below this point, is one half of the data.
•  Third quartile, or upper quartile (Q3): below this point is three quarters of the data.

---

  Quartiles

 Interquartile Range = Q3- Q1 = ((39+46)/2) - ((20+21)/2) = 21

The Interquartile Range is a BASIC measure of spread of the data
