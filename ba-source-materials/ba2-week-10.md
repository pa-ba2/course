1. Types and formats of communication

---

How might you be asked to share your analysis?

  Presentation  Report
 Purpose  Engage audience and convey specific  Provide detailed account of information
  message  and findings

 Format  Verbal with visual aids  Document (written/visual)

 Structure  Can vary depending on message,  Usually follow standardized structure
  employs storytelling techniques

---

Example structure of a business report

  1.  Executive summary: concise take-home
  message of whole report
  2.  Introduction: context and motivation
  3.  Main body: results with supporting
  evidence
  a.  Can be composed of sections for each conclusion
  b.  Include data visualizations or figures to support
  claims
  4.  Conclusion: recommendations, next steps
  5.  Appendices: additional data, detailed
  methods

---

Example of a business presentation

  1.  Engage audience by setting up the
  challenge or sharing interesting finding
  2.  Agenda or outline
  3.  Main content slides
  a.  Use minimal text and strong visuals
  4.  Key takeaways or recommendations
  5.  Conclusion referring back to introduction
  6.  Appendix slides to refer to in case of
  questions

2. Elements of data visualization

---

In presentations and reports, data visualization is key

The charts and graphs you use to analyze your data might not be the best ones to
communicate your results!

Data visualization in presentations should directly support your claims:

---

Ensure graphs are clear, readable, and not misleading:

  When presenting a graph, tell
  the audience:
  -  What does each axis
  represent?
  -  What does each line, point, or
  color represent?
  -  What is the takeaway from the
  data?

3. Tips and tricks

---

Tips and tricks for communicating your analysis

 -  The order you did things usually not the best order to tell things!
 -  Tailor your approach to your audience
 -  Provide context for numbers: how do they compare to an average or known
  reference?
 -  Avoid using acronyms in text or speech
 -  Be prepared for questions at any point during a presentation
