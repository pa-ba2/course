Measure of Central Tendency

●  Mode: The most frequently occurring value in your data set
●  Mean: The average value in the data set
●  Median: The middle value in the data set

Measure of Dispersion

●  Absolute Deviation: The average distance of all of the elements of the elements
  in data set from the mean of the same data set
●  Variance: A measurement of spread between numbers in a dataset
●  Standard Deviation: The Square Root of variance
●  Quartiles: Quartiles separate the data set in to quarters, or quartiles
●  Range: The difference between the largest and the smallest observation in the
  data

What is Business Metrics?

  Standardized quantitative measurements used to track, assess, and analyze speciﬁc data,
  performance, or conditions in various ﬁelds and industries.

Key Beneﬁts of Tracking Business Metrics

  Performance improvement: Tracking the right business metrics tells you how well or poorly
  the business is doing and provides direction for how to improve operations

  Identifying problems: Analyzing business metrics can help identify emerging problems in
  time to correct them before they become major pain points.

  Comparative analysis: Tracking business metrics reveals whether the business is over- or
  underperforming on key industry benchmarks.

  Communication: Reporting business metrics is a vital communications tool for customers,
  shareholders, employees or society at large.

Example

You sell homemade jams at a local farmer's market. In the month of May, you sold the
following:

  Strawberry Jam Jars:  Blueberry Jam Jars:  Peach Jam Jars:
  Selling Price: $5/jar  Selling Price: $6/jar  Selling Price: $4/jar
  Cost: $2/jar  Cost: $3/jar  Cost: $1/jar
  Unit Sold: 100 jars  Unit Sold: 60 jars  Unit Sold: 40 jars

Calculate the following: Total Revenue, Total Cost of Goods Sold, Total Proﬁt

  Total Revenue = (5*100) + (6*60) + (4*40) = 500 + 360 + 160 = $1,020
  Total Cost of Goods Sold = (2*100) + (3*60) + (1*40) = 200 + 180 + 40 = $420
  Total Proﬁt = $1,020 - $420 = $600
